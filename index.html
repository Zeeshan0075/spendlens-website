Auto-capture

Pay the way you always do.
Let the typing happen by itself.

Tap your phone at a till, get a text from your bank, or let your bank's app post an alert — any of them can become a logged expense. Set it up once with a Shortcuts automation: no bank login, no account linking, no aggregator holding your credentials. You can also paste a message, share one to SpendLens, or hand it a screenshot.

Tap to pay

Tap your phone at a till and the purchase appears on its own, with the shop's real name. Your Wallet tap is read on your device, sent nowhere — it even works offline.

Bank alerts

Your bank's text messages — or, on iOS 27, the notifications its app posts, which catch online and in-app purchases too. Screenshots cover older iOS.

Counted once

When the tap, the notification and the text all report one purchase, it's added once. Two real purchases at the same shop stay two.

Built for how banks in the UAE, Oman, Saudi, and across the GCC actually work. Money coming in — salary, a refund — is never added as an expense, and transfers with no shop behind them are left out unless you ask to review them. Nothing is captured until you set up an automation — here's exactly what happens to the text.

Once

Set up an automation in Apple's Shortcuts app — for Apple Pay, for your bank's alerts, or both. SpendLens walks you through it.

Every time

A purchase happens — a tap, a text, an alert. The amount, merchant, date, and currency are pulled out and lined up in your Expenses timeline.

One tap

Tap ✓ to confirm — or swipe it away. Nothing is added without your say-so, unless you switch on automatic adding for the clear ones.

The promise

Your data stays on your device.

No accounts. No analytics SDKs. No ads. No bank credentials — ever. Everything you log lives on your iPhone, with sync through your own private iCloud container.

One feature is different, and we'd rather tell you than bury it: Smart Parsing, which reads your bank's text messages, its app's notifications, and screenshots, sends that text out to be parsed and sends it straight back. It's off until you turn it on, and our endpoint never writes it to a log or a database. Apple Pay purchases are read on your device and never sent anywhere. How Smart Parsing works →
